# Human-style playtest of Pokémon Arena

Tested the packaged build `battle-v5-6dd79c51828d` in fresh local Chromium profiles. Seven LLM-operated perspectives used visible controls for play: a first-time phone player, a story player on a laptop, a collector, a competitive player, a returning offline player, a keyboard-oriented player, and a small-screen visual reviewer. Devices were represented by CSS viewports and input profiles. Players made their own battle decisions and played out the wins and losses. Source and diagnostic inspection followed the relevant observations.

Coverage included the first rival, the first road and city, collecting without an open party slot, PC and item use, the opening Open Circle episode, competitive imports and turns in Gen 9 and Gen 1, and a genuine offline saved-game and backup recovery. This pass covers a fresh player’s opening and selected systems. The previous campaign automation covers later chapters using controlled outcomes for long fights. LLM-operated sessions give reproducible interface evidence and hypotheses about what a person might do. Pacing, difficulty perception, timing, and enjoyment need feedback from people playing unassisted. [Scripted campaign test](PLAYER_PLAYTEST.md)

## How to run a useful LLM playtest

1. **Give the player a goal and a constraint.** Ask a newcomer to pick a partner and find their first battle, or ask a returning player to recover a run without internet. Avoid naming the buttons or sharing a walkthrough. Use separate profiles for newcomers, collectors, experienced battlers, and players who rely on keyboard or enlarged text. Give sessions a clear stopping point, including a natural decision to quit. [Session-Based Test Management](https://www.satisfice.us/articles/sbtm.pdf) · [Usability Testing 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/)
2. **Keep the LLM’s view faithful to the player’s view.** Use screenshots and real input. Add the accessibility tree for a screen-reader or keyboard charter. Withhold source, hidden game state, future objectives, automated wins, and the test harness’s knowledge of where buttons are. A separate diagnostic run can use checkpoints, source, and logs once the issue has been observed. [VideoGameBench](https://vgbench.com/) · [SimUser](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642481)
3. **Record decisions and results as they happen.** For a meaningful action capture the screen, player expectation, action, and outcome. Count misclicks, backtracks, failed attempts, and task outcomes by session. Save the build, viewport, input method, current save or seed where available, and steps to reproduce. Record tool latency separately from player timing. In the Stargaze minigame, screenshot and tool delays consumed part of the real countdown, so that attempt supports feedback about the instruction and visible controls only. [VideoGameBench timing modes](https://vgbench.com/)
4. **Verify important findings from a fresh run.** Let a second tester repeat the symptom without the solution. Move confirmed behavior into a regression that measures the actual outcome, such as comparing the starter’s previewed moves to the moves in its first battle. Keep that regression alongside a fresh exploratory run to see whether the fix reads clearly. GBQA’s authors reported that the strongest tested agent found 48.39% of the verified bugs in their benchmark, which illustrates why uneventful model sessions cannot establish comprehensive coverage. [GBQA workshop paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02648)
5. **Calibrate with real players.** Give a few newcomers and veterans the same goals, start them on their usual devices, and record where they ask for help, give up, or play again. Compare their decisions to the model traces before treating model feedback as audience evidence. Use people’s experience to judge pacing, reactions, and whether the game is fun. [Usability Testing 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/)

### Example tester brief

> You are new to this game and know the basic Pokémon types. Start with a fresh phone profile. Your goal is to choose a partner, learn enough to fight the rival, and reach the first town. Use only what you see or what a normal input would reveal. Play each battle through its actual outcome. Before an important or confusing action, record what you expect in one sentence. If you would normally stop after a failure, stop and record what made you give up. Save screenshots and exact steps for any behavior you can reproduce. Report ease, excitement, and confusion as observations from this run.

# 03 — Fan Fiction & Fan Theories: What Sticks and Why

> Writing-craft dossier for the game's meta time-loop arc ("you are The First, reliving the journey, leaving breadcrumbs for the next loop"). For each entry: the THEME and the NARRATIVE LEVER — why it sticks in players' heads. Claims are tagged **[CANON-SUPPORTED]**, **[POPULAR THEORY]**, or **[DENIED/NON-CANON]**. Cited where non-obvious.

---

## 1. The Great Pokémon War theory

**Status: [POPULAR THEORY] built on one [CANON-SUPPORTED] seed.**

- **The single canon seed:** Lt. Surge (Vermilion Gym) says electric Pokémon saved his life "during the war." That line is real in-game dialogue. ([Bulbapedia – Lt. Surge](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lt._Surge))
- **What's invented:** the war is *never named, dated, or depicted* anywhere in canon — no memorials, no battlefields, no second reference. The "Great Pokémon War / Kanto War" name, the weaponized Pokémon, the wiped-out adult generation, Red's and Blue's dead fathers, and the biker-gang-as-demobilized-soldiers reading are all fan extrapolation hung off that one sentence. ([ScreenRant – War theory explained](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-war-theory-explained-red-blue-no-parents/); [TheGamer – Which war did Lt. Surge fight in?](https://www.thegamer.com/pokemon-lt-surge-american-veteran-war-con/))
- **Counter-evidence fans ignore:** Surge isn't even from Kanto, so his war need not be a Kanto war at all. ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/pokemon-lt-surge-american-veteran-war-con/))

**THEME:** absence as evidence — a world quietly rebuilt on a buried trauma.
**NARRATIVE LEVER:** *the load-bearing single line.* One concrete, unexplained detail (an NPC saying "the war") licenses the player to retro-fit an entire dark backstory onto everything that was previously just unexplained design (no dads, kids alone, listless adults). The theory sticks because it makes the *gaps* feel intentional. **Directly transferable to a loop arc:** one stray, slightly-wrong line ("haven't we... done this before?") can retroactively reframe the whole game as evidence of the loop.

## 2. Cubone / Marowak / Kangaskhan grief lore

**Status: mixed — grief is [CANON-SUPPORTED]; the Kangaskhan link is [POPULAR THEORY].**

- **Canon:** Cubone's Pokédex entries state it wears the skull of its *dead mother* and cries at night. In Pokémon Tower (Lavender), a Marowak that died protecting its child from Team Rocket haunts the building and must be fought as a ghost. Both are explicitly in-game. ([Wikipedia – Cubone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubone))
- **Unconfirmed fan theory:** that Cubone is a baby **Kangaskhan**, orphaned, wearing its mother's skull — an evolutionary line cut for being too grim. Supported only by visual resemblance (head shape, body, tail); The Pokémon Company has never confirmed it. ([ScreenRant – Cubone/Kangaskhan](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-theory-cubone-kangaskhan-mother-child-confirmed-evolution/); [Bogleech – beta Marowak](https://bogleech.com/pokemon/pbeta146marowak))

**THEME:** inherited grief made literal — the child *wears* the dead parent and carries it forward.
**NARRATIVE LEVER:** *the canon detail that's already disturbing, then escalated.* The base fact (a skull mask that is a real corpse) is canon and self-contained; the theory's pull is wanting that horror to mean *more*. For a loop arc, note the structural rhyme: a successor literally wearing/carrying what the predecessor left behind is the same image as "The First leaving breadcrumbs the next loop inherits."

## 3. Mr. Fuji = Dr. Fuji / the Amber arc

**Status: anime-movie-adjacent, NOT game canon; the Amber arc is now [DENIED/NON-CANON].**

- **Game canon:** Mr. Fuji is a kindly old man who runs the Lavender volunteer Pokémon house caring for orphaned Pokémon. That's all the games say. ([Bulbapedia – Mr. Fuji](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mr._Fuji))
- **Anime film:** *Dr. Fuji* in *Mewtwo Strikes Back* (1998) is a scientist who cloned Mewtwo while trying to resurrect his dead daughter **Amber** (Ambertwo). The "Mr. Fuji = Dr. Fuji" identification was only stated outside the films, around 2013 — never explicit in the games. ([Bulbapedia – Dr. Fuji](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Dr._Fuji); [ScreenRant – who created Mewtwo](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-mewtwo-creator-red-blue-game-world/))
- **De-canonization:** the 2019 remake *Mewtwo Strikes Back — Evolution* **removed the Amber storyline entirely**, so the anime universe no longer treats it as canon. ([Bulbapedia – Mr. Fuji](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mr._Fuji))

**THEME:** the grieving creator who tries to cheat death and produces a monster.
**NARRATIVE LEVER:** *cross-text inference (and its fragility).* Fans bridged two media to build a richer character — and the bridge collapsed when a remake retconned it. Lesson for the game: a backstory assembled from inference is powerful but *deniable*; if you want a reveal to last, anchor it in something the game itself owns rather than a connection the audience has to import.

## 4. Ash's Coma theory

**Status: [DENIED/NON-CANON] — never addressed by Game Freak; persists anyway.**

- **The theory:** Ash entered a coma early on (the bike crash / Pikachu's first big shock), and the entire series since is his wish-fulfillment dream — which is why he never ages and the world is impossibly kind to his ambition. ([ScreenRant – Ash coma](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-theory-ash-coma-doesnt-age-grow-up/); [TV Tropes – Ash's Coma](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Fanfic/AshsComa))
- **Why it won't die:** it's the most *emotionally economical* answer to a genuine anomaly — Ash's frozen age. The audience grows up; Ash doesn't; the brain wants one explanation that resolves the dissonance, and "it's all a dream" is the cheapest one available. ([ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-theory-ash-coma-doesnt-age-grow-up/))
- **Reality check:** the creators have never hinted at it; the agelessness is a marketing/animation continuity artifact, not a plot. ([ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-theory-ash-coma-doesnt-age-grow-up/))

**THEME:** the endless journey as denial — the dream you can't wake from.
**NARRATIVE LEVER:** *the anomaly that demands an explanation the text refuses to give.* The theory is parasitic on an unexplained structural quirk (he never ages / time doesn't progress). **This is the single most relevant precedent for a loop arc:** "the protagonist is trapped, reliving, never moving forward" is *exactly* the Ash-coma intuition — except a loop game can do what the anime won't: confirm it, and turn the dissonance from a fan bug into the actual text.

## 5. "The adults are absent / children run the world"

**Status: thematic reading; partly [CANON-SUPPORTED] absence, partly [DENIED/NON-CANON] explanation.**

- **Canon observation:** in Red/Blue you never meet your father (one offhand mention at most); ten-year-olds set off alone; adults are oddly passive. ([ScreenRant – the one game with a dad](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-game-protagonist-dad-father-where-norman/); [TheGamer – unresolved mysteries](https://www.thegamer.com/unresolved-mysteries-and-plot-holes-pokemon-red-blue-and-yellow-left-hanging/))
- **Likely real reason (design, not theme):** Game Freak only modeled family members with a *mechanical* job — Mom heals you, Oak gives starters, Daisy gives the Town Map. Absent fathers had no function, so they weren't built. ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/15-crazy-things-you-didnt-know-about-pokemon-red-and-blue/))
- **Shudo's age-10-=-adult detail [DENIED/NON-CANON]:** anime head writer **Takeshi Shudo**, in his two-volume novelization *Pocket Monsters: The Animation*, wrote that in this world people are *legally adults at 10* — they pay tax, can be arrested, can marry and work, because compulsory schooling ends then. This explains kids journeying alone, but it is the *novelization*, contradicts the anime in places, and is **not canon**. ([Bulbagarden forum – ten-year-olds as adults](https://bulbagarden.net/threads/are-ten-year-olds-considered-adults-in-the-pokemon-world.282964/); [TV Tropes – Pocket Monsters: The Animation](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/PocketMonstersTheAnimation))

**THEME:** a world handed to children — autonomy that reads as freedom in daylight and abandonment at night.
**NARRATIVE LEVER:** *functional absence reread as meaningful absence.* Players take a production shortcut (no dad-NPC) and convert it into mood. The lever is *negative space*: what the game *doesn't* show invites the player to author the dread themselves — far stickier than spelling it out. A loop arc can weaponize the same negative space (why is the town always *just* slightly emptier than it should be?).

## 6. Time-loop / New Game+ / "the player is a god replaying" — the meta gold standards

**Status: craft analysis of [shipped works].** This is the core reference for the game's loop arc.

### The meta/loop reading of Pokémon itself
Every Pokémon save is already a loop machine: New Game+ "offers the simple pleasure of replaying a game you've fallen in love with, only now you know where everything is" — yet the convention is that *characters never remember*, and are told "You Shouldn't Know This Already." Save-scumming reloads make the *player*, not the trainer, the only entity who remembers across resets. ([TV Tropes – New Game Plus](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/NewGamePlus); [TV Tropes – Save Scumming](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SaveScumming)) The game's premise ("you are The First, reliving, leaving breadcrumbs") simply *makes the trainer share the player's memory* — collapsing the usual gap. That collapse is the whole trick.

### What makes a time-loop reveal LAND (general craft)
- **The 80/20 anchor rule:** ~80% of a loop can drift and mutate, but ~20% — one or two elements — must stay *absolutely constant*, or the audience reads randomness instead of repetition. The constant is what signals "this is a loop, on purpose." ([Medium – making sci-fi loops work](https://medium.com/@info_13818/the-eternal-return-making-sci-fi-loops-work-in-ai-filmmaking-1b7dbfc9ca7b))
- **Each cycle must reveal one new piece.** "If nothing meaningful changes between iterations, that's not a loop; that's a GIF." The loop is a puzzle that hands over one piece per pass. ([authorcarlara – how to depict a time loop](https://www.authorcarlara.com/post/how-to-depict-a-time-loop))
- **Confined space builds shared knowledge.** Once the audience knows the space cold, *anything new or different* screams for attention — recurring elements become touchstones that trigger inferences about change. ([authorcarlara](https://www.authorcarlara.com/post/how-to-depict-a-time-loop); [Hyperrhiz – narrative potentials of the loop](https://hyperrhiz.io/hyperrhiz22/essays/2-luers-narrative-potentials.html))

### Undertale — SAVE / Genocide / Pacifist
- **Technique:** the in-fiction SAVE power is canonized as a *character ability* (Flowey/Sans had it first), so reloading isn't cheating outside the story — it's an act *inside* the story you're accountable for. Flowey: "I know what you did. You murdered them. And then you went back, because you regretted it." A Genocide run permanently scars *all* future routes, even after a True Reset. ([The Boar – Undertale retrospective](https://theboar.org/2025/02/ethics-consequence-and-player-agency-an-undertale-retrospective/); [Undertale Wiki – Genocide Route](https://undertale.fandom.com/wiki/Genocide_Route))
- **Why it lands:** it makes *save/reload* — the player's god-power — diegetic and morally costed. **Most directly applicable lever:** "leaving breadcrumbs for the next loop" is the benevolent twin of Undertale's "your past runs haunt this one." Persistent state across resets = the reveal has teeth.

### Doki Doki Literature Club
- **Technique:** the antagonist (Monika) is aware she's in a game, edits/deletes the *other characters' files*, and progression requires the **player to delete `monika.chr` from their own disk** — making the player complicit. It reads the player's username/files to feel like it has escaped into reality. ([Bloody Disgusting – fourth-wall anxiety in DDLC](https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3559587/craft-fourth-wall-breaking-anxiety-doki-doki-literature-club/); [withaterriblefate – sadistic horror in DDLC](https://withaterriblefate.com/2018/04/13/not-just-monika-sadistic-horror-in-doki-doki-literature-club/))
- **Why it lands:** it relocates the fourth wall — *the player is now inside the game's economy and a character acts on them from outside.* Lever for a loop: the entity that "remembers" can address the *player's* persistence, not just the character's.

### OMORI
- **Technique:** the dream-world (Headspace) and the alter-ego "Omori" are the protagonist Sunny's *repression engine*; the truth (he caused Mari's death) leaks out through environmental tells until the player must choose Omori (denial) or Sunny (acceptance). The black lightbulb literally visualizes "the repression of an idea," pulsing as truth nears. ([Into The Spine – White Space](https://intothespine.com/2021/02/26/omori-white-space/); [Wikipedia – Omori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omori_(video_game)))
- **Why it lands:** the *form* (a too-clean, looping safe space) *is* the theme (repression). Lever for a loop: make the loop itself feel like a comfort the protagonist built to avoid something — so escaping it costs.

### NieR: Automata — Ending E
- **Technique:** after the final ending, the game asks the player to **permanently delete their own save** to send anonymous help to a stranger struggling with the same boss. Sacrifice wipes all progress; the only "reward" is a changed title screen and someone else's run rescued. ([Kotaku – difficult decision at the end](https://kotaku.com/theres-a-difficult-decision-at-the-end-of-nier-automat-1793071026); [iThrive – true kindness requires sacrifice](https://ithrivegames.org/newsroom/nier_automata_true_kindness_requires_sacrifice/))
- **Why it lands:** it turns the *save file* — the player's accumulated self — into a gift to a future stranger. **This is almost literally the game's premise:** "leaving breadcrumbs for the next loop" = sacrificing/seeding your run so the *next* traveler benefits. NieR proves players feel this profoundly.

### The Stanley Parable
- **Technique:** the Narrator describes your actions *before* you take them, daring disobedience; every rebellion turns out to be another authored branch. The game does "anti-design" — exposing that player agency is a curated performance. ([Medium/Comeau – illusion of choice](https://medium.com/@joshpcomeau/the-illusion-of-choice-how-the-stanley-parable-reveals-the-limits-of-player-agency-in-game-design-51c50090964e); [Wikipedia – The Stanley Parable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Parable))
- **Why it lands:** it names the loop player and designer are already in — "you are walking a predetermined path and calling it freedom." Lever for a loop arc: the protagonist realizing the journey is *authored/repeating* mirrors the player realizing the *game* is — fuse the two and the reveal hits on both layers at once.

## 7. Acclaimed long-form Pokémon fanfic — what each is praised for at the craft level

### Pokémon: The Origin of Species (Daystar Eld) — [rationalist fic]
- **Praised for:** internal logical consistency — taking the contradictory Pokémon world and making it *cohere* as a slightly-smarter-than-ours society that actually acted on its research; and for *arc climaxes* reviewers rank beside *Worm* and *A Practical Guide to Evil*. Inspired by *HPMOR*; its psychology is built on real research. ([TV Tropes – Origin of Species](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Fanfic/PokemonTheOriginOfSpecies); [Substack review](https://stiangronlund.substack.com/p/short-review-of-pokemon-the-origin); [author site](https://daystareld.com/pokemon/))
- **Craft lesson:** *consistency as a load-bearing pleasure.* A world that rewards the player for thinking is itself a hook. For a loop game: the loop rules must be *consistent enough to reason about* — fans must be able to predict and exploit them.

### Traveler (The Straight Elf) — [AU retelling]
- **Praised for:** characterization at scale — ~2.4M words / 87 chapters where *every* character (canon, OC, human, Pokémon) has a distinct personality built through "the most subtle of hints and the most gradual of characterizations." A traumatized, quiet, mature Ash; Pokémon who read as *people*, not movesets. ([TV Tropes – Traveler](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Fanfic/Traveler); [TV Tropes reviews](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/reviews.php?target_group=Fanfic&target_title=Traveler))
- **Craft lesson:** *gradual, hint-based characterization survives huge length.* For a loop game where you re-meet the same cast every cycle, the payoff is letting the player notice tiny changes in characters they thought they knew — the loop turns "subtle characterization" into a gameplay verb.

### Ashes of the Past (Saphroneth) — [loop / "Peggy Sue" fic — the key model]
- **Premise:** Cyrus remade reality; Arceus sends **Ash back to day one of his journey** with the power to restore his friends' memories of the lost timeline, to fix as much as possible before the world ends. ([TV Tropes – Ashes of the Past](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Fanfic/AshesOfThePast); [AO3 series](https://archiveofourown.org/series/3395512))
- **Praised for:** the creative time-travel engine and the *dramatic irony of foreknowledge* — the tension of knowing what's coming and racing to change it. (It's also long-running and famously *unfinished*, a cautionary note on loop-fic pacing.)
- **Craft lesson — the closest structural sibling to this game:** foreknowledge + selectively shared memory is the engine. The fun is (a) the protagonist knowing the script, (b) *choosing whom to let in on it*, and (c) the world drifting off-script as changes compound. "Leaving breadcrumbs for the next loop" is this same machinery, pointed forward instead of backward.

### Honest labels (do not inflate)
- **"A Grand Day Out" — [ROM HACK, not prose].** A short GBA LeafGreen mini-hack by Cutlerine (built for a PokéCommunity speed-hacking contest): explosions on Route 1, Team Rocket hunting a Cubone, you can dress as a Rocket grunt. Cubone-themed, but it is a *playable hack*, not acclaimed fanfiction — don't cite it as long-form prose. ([PokéCommunity thread](https://www.pokecommunity.com/threads/a-grand-day-out.276201/); [PokéHarbor](https://www.pokeharbor.com/2021/11/pokemon-a-grand-day-out/))
- **"Latency" — [UNDER-INDEXED / UNVERIFIED].** No reliable, attributable record surfaced for a notable Pokémon fic by this title; general searches return only platform indexes, not the work. **Do not invent praise or plot for it.** If referenced, label it as unverified and locate the actual author/URL before relying on it.

## Levers that make theories & meta-narratives land

Synthesis. ⟳ = especially relevant to a time-loop reveal.

1. **The single load-bearing detail.** One concrete, slightly-anomalous fact (Surge's "the war"; Cubone's real-skull mask) licenses the audience to build a whole structure. *Plant one undeniable, slightly-wrong detail rather than explaining the loop.* ⟳
2. **Negative space as authorship.** What you *don't* show (absent fathers, empty towns) lets players author the dread — stickier than exposition. *Let the loop reveal itself through absence first.* ⟳
3. **The anomaly that demands explanation.** A persistent unexplained quirk (Ash never ages) breeds theories the text never sanctioned. *A loop game can confirm what the anomaly implies — turning a "bug" intuition into canon.* ⟳
4. **Diegetic save/reset.** The player's god-powers (save, reload, New Game+) become *in-story* and morally costed (Undertale; DDLC's file deletion; NieR's save sacrifice). *Make remembering-across-resets a character trait, not a menu function.* ⟳
5. **The 80/20 loop anchor + one new piece per cycle.** Keep ~20% constant so repetition reads as intentional; reveal exactly one new piece each pass so it's a puzzle, not a GIF. ⟳
6. **The form is the theme.** OMORI's too-safe dream *is* repression; Stanley's branching *is* false freedom. *Make the loop itself feel like a comfort/prison the protagonist built — so leaving it costs something.* ⟳
7. **Relocate the fourth wall.** Don't just break it — put the player *inside* the fiction's economy while something addresses them from outside (DDLC, NieR-E). *The entity that "remembers the loop" should be able to speak to the player's persistence, not only the character's.* ⟳
8. **Sacrifice forward to a stranger.** NieR-E proves players feel deeply about seeding a *future* run at the cost of their own. *"Breadcrumbs for the next loop" is exactly this — make the cost real and the beneficiary a stranger (or your next self).* ⟳
9. **Foreknowledge + selectively shared memory.** *Ashes of the Past*'s engine: the protagonist knows the script and chooses whom to let in, while changes make the world drift off-script. ⟳
10. **Cross-text inference is powerful but deniable.** Fuji/Amber shows inference-built lore can be retconned away; anchor a reveal in what the game *itself* owns if you want it to last.
11. **Consistency is a pleasure in itself.** *Origin of Species* — a world the player can reason about and exploit is a hook. *Loop rules must be predictable enough to be gamed.*
12. **Gradual, hint-based characterization scales and rewards return.** *Traveler* — across a long/looping work, tiny consistent changes in familiar characters become the payoff. ⟳

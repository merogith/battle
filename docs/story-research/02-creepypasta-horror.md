# 02 — Creepypasta & Fan-Horror: The Mechanics of Pokémon Dread

> Dossier note: A writing-craft reference for the horror/uncanny "extra" story pool. Each entry leads with the HORROR MECHANIC (how it scares), not the plot. Claims are tagged **[REAL/IN-GAME]** (actual ROM/game content), **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** (the legend that accreted around it), or **[CANON LORE]** (official Pokédex/story canon). Every non-obvious claim is cited; URLs collected under Sources.

---

## Lavender Town Syndrome — *the cursed-sound legend*

**Horror mechanic: real-world contagion via the speaker grille.** The most reusable trick here is that the horror is not *inside* the fiction — it claims to leak *out* of the cartridge into your body. The legend says a frequency only children can hear induced headaches, nosebleeds, and a wave of child suicides in 1996 Japan. The dread comes from "the thing you already played as a kid was secretly hurting you," weaponizing a piece of media the audience trusts and remembers fondly.

- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** The "hundreds of child suicides" story is a 2010s creepypasta, first seeded on Pastebin (Feb 21, 2010) and spread via 4chan. No deaths, no syndrome, no medical record exists. ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/lavender-town-syndrome-creepypasta), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavender_Town))
- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** "Binaural beats / high-frequency tones brainwashed children." Binaural beats are a sub-~30 Hz perceived "beat" from two close tones; they cannot encode commands and cannot operate as a brainwashing carrier above ~1 kHz. The Game Boy's mono speaker also can't produce true binaural separation. The "proof" — spectrograms with Unown or ghost faces hidden in them — were **Photoshopped after the fact**. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavender_Town), [Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/pokemon-lavender-town-history-haunted-urban-legend-creepypasta-explained/))
- **[REAL/IN-GAME]** The theme *is* genuinely dissonant by design — composer Junichi Masuda wrote an unsettling, atonal track for the in-game graveyard town. The creepypasta latched onto a real artistic choice. ([OC ReMix](https://ocremix.org/song/7102/theme-of-lavender-town))
- **[REAL/IN-GAME]** The Gen II recomposition is real: in Gold/Silver/Crystal (and HeartGold/SoulSilver) the Lavender Town theme was rearranged into a notably *cheerier* tune, matching the in-story demolition of Pokémon Tower into the Kanto Radio Tower. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavender_Town), [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Junichi-Masuda/Lavender-Town-(GSC)/))
- **[REAL/IN-GAME, partial]** A genuine technical difference exists between regions: Western Red/Blue contain a real high-pitched component in the track, and fans debate a v1.0 Japanese Red/Green variant. There is *one* canonical Gen I composition; the "Japan had a deadlier mix that was toned down for the West" framing is **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** embellishment built on a sliver of real audio difference. ([Bulbagarden thread](https://bulbagarden.net/threads/lavender-town-music-anomaly.71398/), [Bulbapedia Talk](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Talk:Lavender_Town))
- Plausibility scaffolding: the legend rode the coattails of the **real** 1997 "Dennō Senshi Porygon" anime episode, where strobing red/blue frames triggered seizures in ~700 children — a documented event that made "Pokémon physically harmed kids" feel pre-verified. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavender_Town))

**Reusable craft:** the *out-of-diegesis* threat (the media object harms the player), and "official artifact + one true weird detail + fabricated proof image" as a credibility stack.

## Lost Silver — *the haunted save file*

**Horror mechanic: save-file-as-narrative (diegetic UI corruption).** The story is told almost entirely through the *menus and metadata* of a used Gold/Silver cartridge — trainer name, party, Pokédex completion, the death/faint label. Nothing is narrated by a monster; the horror is reconstructed by the player reading a UI that is *almost* normal and then progressively *wrong*. This is the foundational template for the entire haunted-cartridge subgenre.

- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** Earliest known post: a 4chan thread, June 6, 2010. There is no real haunted cartridge; it's fiction framed as a found-object account. ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/pokemon-lost-silver), [Pokepasta Wiki](https://pokepasta.fandom.com/wiki/Lost_Silver))
- Mechanic catalogue inside the story:
  - **The wrong name.** Trainer is named "…" (ellipsis) — an unspeakable/blank identity. The dead previous owner.
  - **Impossible completion.** Maxed money, levels, and a full 251 Pokédex including Celebi/Mew — wealth and "winning" recoded as a death state.
  - **Acrostic party as a voice.** Five Unown spell **LEAVE**; later messages spell **DYING** and **NO MORE**. The roster *itself* talks. The Cyndaquil is named "HURRY." ([Creepypasta Files Wiki](https://creepypastafiles.fandom.com/wiki/Lost_Silver), [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Fanfic/PokemonLostSilver))
  - **The broken word.** A Pokémon **DIED** instead of fainted — the engine's safe euphemism is stripped away, signaling the rules no longer protect you.
  - **The silent overworld.** A still, music-less Bellsprout Tower; using Flash produces an ominous red glow instead of light — a familiar utility move turned malignant.
  - **The unwinnable / mutual-annihilation battle.** A ghost battle vs. the Red/Blue trainer ends with Celebi + a tormented Pikachu killing each other via Perish Song + Destiny Bond — victory and loss collapse into the same outcome.
  - **The trap ending.** The "…" trainer becomes a ghost among Pokémon graves, looping forever.

**Reusable craft:** tell the story through the *load screen and party menu*; use the death/faint vocabulary swap as a single high-impact reveal; let the party order be a hidden message.

## Pokémon Black / "Creepy Black" — *GHOST and the CURSE button*

**Horror mechanic: the unwinnable encounter that kills *trainers*, not Pokémon, plus a single forbidden verb.** A bootleg Red adds one extra party member: a Level-glitched Pokémon literally named **GHOST**, drawn as the Lavender Town ghost sprite (the one you normally can't see without the Silph Scope). Its only move is **CURSE** — and using it doesn't reduce HP, it makes the *opposing trainer* "vanish." The escalation is moral: you stop battling Pokémon and start erasing people, with no resistance and no fail state.

- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** No such bootleg is known to exist. Surfaced on the Tiny Cartridge DS blog (Jul 27, 2010), boosted by Kotaku ~3 days later; the canonical /x/ (paranormal board) archive dates to Nov 11, 2010. ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/pokemon-creepy-black), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/an-infamous-creepypasta-that-explored-the-dark-side-of-1821228942), [Creepypasta.com](https://www.creepypasta.com/pokemon-black/))
- Escalation ladder: opponents you defeat with GHOST don't faint — their *sprite disappears* and they're gone. By endgame the player has depopulated the world; the final use of CURSE makes the *player character* disappear too. The horror is the slow realization that the "cheat Pokémon" is an erasure tool and you've been using it gladly. ([Creepypasta Wiki](https://creepypasta.fandom.com/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Black), [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Fanfic/PokemonBlack))
- **[REAL/IN-GAME, source material]** The GHOST sprite is real — the unidentifiable ghost shown in Pokémon Tower before you have the Silph Scope. The pasta *literalizes* an existing in-game asset, which is what makes it land. ([Creepypasta Wiki](https://creepypasta.fandom.com/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Black))

**Reusable craft:** give the player *one* power that is overtly too strong and quietly atrocious; make the cost retroactive (you enjoyed it before you understood it); the final target is the player's own avatar.

## Strangled Red — *grief, the long con, and MissingNo. as a resurrection*

**Horror mechanic: the familiar-made-personal, then desecrated.** The trick is to first *over-humanize* the normally silent protagonist — give "Steven" a name, a brother/rival, real dialogue, a beloved Charmander "Miki" — so the player bonds. Then a routine, beloved game action (a **Pokédex trade**) kills Miki. The horror is that the warmth was the setup; the deeper the attachment, the worse the loss.

- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** Posted to DeviantArt, Jan 14, 2011; widely adapted into fan comics. ([Gaming Urban Legends Wiki](https://gaming-urban-legends.fandom.com/wiki/Strangled_Red), [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Fanfic/PokemonStrangledRed))
- Mechanic ladder: bonding → loss-via-normal-mechanic (the trade) → grief-driven misuse of a **real** glitch (MissingNo.) to "bring Miki back" → the resurrection returns *wrong* (a corrupted Miki) → the protagonist's humanity decays → he strangles his brother Mike and becomes the monster. The title is the destination. ([Creepypasta Wiki](https://creepypasta.fandom.com/wiki/Strangled_Red))
- Note the cross-pollination: it borrows the **real** MissingNo. glitch (below) and recasts it as a Monkey's-Paw revival engine — folklore building on a true mechanic.

**Reusable craft:** weaponize attachment — humanize first, then let a *trusted, mundane* mechanic deliver the wound; use a real glitch as a "do not resurrect the dead" device that pays back corrupted.

## Glitchy Red / Buried Alive — *the dread that subverts its own legend*

**Horror mechanic: anti-payoff and second-person address.** Glitchy Red is meta-aware: the narrator *expects* the famous scares (Unown, the "Buried Alive guy") and **nothing supernatural happens** in Lavender Town — the let-down itself is unnerving. The real hit lands when the game stops talking *about* a character and starts talking *to the player by name*: "RED: Nice try, [your name]," in all caps. Party members (Raticate, Pidgeot) then silently vanish. The horror is the game acknowledging *you*.

- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** Glitchy Red — a "bootleg eBay Red" found-object pasta about a corrupted, freezing cart. ([Creepypasta Wiki](https://creepypasta.fandom.com/wiki/Glitchy_Red))
- **[MYTH/FABRICATED] Buried Alive** — the most "technical-sounding" Pokémon legend, framed as *cut developer content*: a "Buryman" script for a boss that supposedly sat atop Pokémon Tower before being replaced by the Marowak ghost. The model is a decaying corpse clawing out of the ground; it begs *"You're... Here. I'm trapped... And I'm lonely... Won't you join me?"* and fights with two **White Hands** + Gengar + Muk. ([Pokepasta Wiki](https://pokepasta.fandom.com/wiki/Buried_Alive_Model), [Creepypasta Files Wiki](https://creepypastafiles.fandom.com/wiki/Buried_Alive))
- **The signature unwinnable mechanic:** *no defeat protocol was written* — beat Buried Alive and the **game freezes**; lose and a unique game-over shows him eating your character while gurgling *"Finally, fresh meat!"* The only "valid" outcome is your death; winning breaks reality (crash). ([Pokepasta Wiki](https://pokepasta.fandom.com/wiki/Buried_Alive_Model))
- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** Despite the "leftover dev asset" framing, Buried Alive was never in any official code or storyline — the cut-content pretext is part of the fiction. ([Creepypasta Files Wiki](https://creepypastafiles.fandom.com/wiki/Buried_Alive))

**Reusable craft:** (1) defy the audience's genre expectations to disorient them, then strike; (2) break the fourth wall by name; (3) the *crash-as-ending* — a "win" that the system can't process, so it just stops, implying you went somewhere off the map.

## Hypno's Lullaby — *the nursery rhyme as predator's POV*

**Horror mechanic: the lullaby form weaponized + the second-person victim.** The terror is structural: a sing-song children's rhyme, the safest possible literary form, is voiced *from the abductor's perspective* and addressed to a child — "come little children, I'll take thee away." The cozy meter is the camouflage; the content is grooming and consumption (Hypno lures children with its pendulum and "feasts on their dreams for eternity").

- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** A creepypasta poem; posted to PokéCommunity Aug 10, 2010 (poster noted it had circulated for months prior); lyrics often attributed to "Steven Winters." Set to the Lavender Town theme, TrainerDerek's sing-spoken YouTube version (Nov 19, 2010) passed 3M views — the *delivery* (childlike vocal over the dirge) is the scare. ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/hypnos-lullaby), [Creepypasta.com](https://www.creepypasta.com/hypnos-lullaby/))
- **[CANON LORE] seed:** It grows directly from a real Pokédex entry. Hypno's FireRed entry: *"It carries a pendulum-like device. There once was an incident in which it took away a child it hypnotized."* The pasta just refuses to look away from a sentence the games stated and moved past. ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/hypnos-lullaby))

**Reusable craft:** put horror in a *safe container* (lullaby, jingle, tutorial voice); write from the predator's first person addressing "you/little one"; build directly off a canon line the audience half-remembers.

## MissingNo. — *the glitch as folk demon*

**Horror mechanic: a real corruption mythologized.** MissingNo. matters as craft because it's the seam where REAL and MYTH meet — and the lesson is that *true* glitches are scarier raw than invented ones, because players can reproduce them.

- **[REAL/IN-GAME] How it actually works:** During the Viridian City old-man tutorial the game temporarily overwrites the *player's name* into a memory buffer it also uses for wild-encounter data. Cinnabar Island's east coast (and Seafoam) has *no dedicated encounter table* — a real programming oversight — so surfing there makes the engine read your *name's bytes* as encounter data. Invalid species IDs render as MissingNo. Which species/levels appear depends on specific characters of your name. ([Bulbapedia: Old man glitch](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Old_man_glitch), [Wikipedia: MissingNo.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MissingNo.), [Glitch City Wiki](https://glitchcity.wiki/wiki/Old_man_glitch))
- **[REAL/IN-GAME] Side effects:** encountering it duplicates the 6th inventory item (+128) — the famous "Rare Candy / Master Ball" duplication — and can corrupt the Hall of Fame and graphics. A genuine, documented, reproducible artifact. ([Wikipedia: MissingNo.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MissingNo.), [The Cutting Room Floor](https://tcrf.net/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Blue/MissingNo.))
- **[MYTH/FABRICATED]** "MissingNo. is secretly Mew," "catching it wipes/bricks your cartridge forever," "it's a censored/forbidden Pokémon." Folklore. The duplication and graphical glitching are real; permanent bricking and the Mew identity are not. ([Wikipedia: MissingNo.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MissingNo.))

**Reusable craft:** the most credible horror is *reproducible* — give the player a "do this exact sequence and the world breaks" ritual; let the corruption be tied to *their own name/data* so it feels personal and earned, not authored.

## Canon dread species — *real Pokédex lore taken literally*

**Horror mechanic: the games already wrote the horror; the craft is refusing the franchise's tonal off-ramp.** Mainline Pokédex entries contain genuinely grim lore, usually buffered by cute sprites and upbeat music. Fan-horror's most honest move is simply to *believe the entry* and remove the buffer.

- **[CANON LORE] Drifloon** — "the Signpost for Wandering Spirits." Sinnoh dex: it *"tugs on the hands of children to steal them away"*; children are said to vanish; bursting Drifloon spill a screaming soul. A balloon that abducts kids. ([CBR](https://www.cbr.com/pokemon-pokedex-creepy/), [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/pokemon-ghost-type-messed-up-pokedex/))
- **[CANON LORE] Yamask** — Black dex: each carries *"a mask that used to be its face when it was human."* It is a dead person; it weeps looking at its old face. The franchise canonizes humans-become-Pokémon-after-death. ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-yamask-origin-backstory-dead-human-ghost-explained/), [Pokemon.com](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/yamask))
- **[CANON LORE] Phantump** — "old tales say [they] are stumps possessed by the spirits of children who died lost in the forest." They mimic a crying child to lure people — and get *those* people lost and killed too. A self-replicating tragedy. ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/article/21818-spooky-pokemon-halloween-pokedex-entry), [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/pokemon-scariest-pokedex-entries-surprisingly-dark/))
- **[CANON LORE] Banette** — an abandoned doll that "amassed a grudge"; hatred animated it; it haunts trash heaps hunting the child who threw it away, and stabs *itself* with pins to curse that child. ([Pokemon.com](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/banette), [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/pokemon-anime-bannete-pokedex-entry-chilling/))
- **[CANON LORE] Parasect** — body-horror, not ghost: the mushroom on its back has *drained and now controls* the dead bug host; remove the mushroom and Parasect stops moving entirely. A puppeteered corpse — Pokémon's Cordyceps "zombie-ant" (each Paras→Parasect evolution is arguably a killing). ([Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Parasect_(Pok%C3%A9mon)), [Pokemon.com](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/parasect))
- **[CANON LORE] Giratina / Distortion World** — the canonical "wrong-side" setting: Arceus banished Giratina (officially the personification of **antimatter**, per Masuda/Kawachimaru) to the **Distortion World**, the reverse side of reality where *time does not flow and space is unstable*. A ready-made uncanny space sanctioned by canon. ([Bulbapedia: Giratina](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Giratina_(Pok%C3%A9mon)), [Bulbapedia: Distortion World](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Distortion_World))
- **[CANON LORE] Lavender Tower (Pokémon Tower)** — a literal Pokémon graveyard. In canon, Team Rocket invades to harvest **Cubone skulls** for profit; one **Marowak mother is killed** defending her child, and her **ghost** blocks the top floor until you obtain the Silph Scope to *see* her — then her orphaned Cubone arrives and she passes on. Death, grief, profit-driven murder, and a ghost you can't perceive without the right item — all shipped, all canon. ([Bulbapedia: Marowak (ghost)](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Marowak_(ghost)), [Bulbapedia: Pokémon Tower](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Tower))

**Reusable craft:** don't invent dark lore — *take the existing entry at face value* and strip the cute presentation; the dissonance between adorable design and stated fate is the engine. The "ghost you need a special item to see" (Silph Scope) is a canon perception-horror device you can reuse directly.

## Catalog of horror mechanics

| # | Mechanic | Best exemplar(s) | One-line craft note |
|---|----------|------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | **Familiar-made-wrong** | All; esp. Lavender music, Flash→red glow (Lost Silver) | Take a trusted asset/move and twist *one* property. |
| 2 | **Save-file / diegetic-UI corruption as narrative** | Lost Silver, Glitchy Red | Tell the story through menus, party order, metadata. |
| 3 | **The silent overworld** | Lost Silver | Remove music/NPCs; stillness reads as wrongness. |
| 4 | **The death/faint vocabulary swap** | Lost Silver ("DIED") | Strip the engine's protective euphemism for one reveal. |
| 5 | **Party/roster as a hidden message (acrostic)** | Lost Silver (LEAVE / DYING / NO MORE) | Let game data spell the threat. |
| 6 | **Escalating wrongness toward a named destination** | Strangled Red, Pokémon Black | The title/ending is the arc's terminus; build to it. |
| 7 | **The second-person victim / fourth-wall address** | Glitchy Red ("Nice try, [name]"), Hypno's Lullaby | The game/poem speaks to *you*, by name. |
| 8 | **The unwinnable encounter** | Buried Alive, Lost Silver (Perish Song + Destiny Bond) | Only valid outcome is your loss; win = mutual death or crash. |
| 9 | **Crash-as-ending** | Buried Alive (no defeat protocol) | A "win" the system can't process → it just stops. |
| 10 | **One forbidden verb / too-strong power with retroactive cost** | Pokémon Black (CURSE / GHOST) | Give a power that's overtly OP and quietly atrocious. |
| 11 | **Resurrection-gone-wrong via real glitch** | Strangled Red + MissingNo. | Folklore on top of a true mechanic; the dead come back corrupted. |
| 12 | **Out-of-diegesis / real-world contagion** | Lavender Town Syndrome | Claim the media object harms the player's body. |
| 13 | **Reproducible-ritual horror** | MissingNo. | "Do this exact sequence and the world breaks" — tied to player's own data/name. |
| 14 | **Safe-container subversion** | Hypno's Lullaby (lullaby form) | Hide horror in the safest format and POV. |
| 15 | **Real-Pokédex-lore-turned-literal** | Drifloon, Yamask, Phantump, Banette, Parasect | Believe the entry; remove the cute buffer. |
| 16 | **Perception-horror item** | Lavender Tower / Silph Scope (canon); GHOST sprite | A ghost only visible with the right item. |
| 17 | **Sanctioned uncanny space** | Distortion World (canon) | A reverse-side dimension where physics is wrong, no invention needed. |
| 18 | **Credibility stack: official artifact + one true weird detail + fabricated proof** | Lavender Town Syndrome, Buried Alive | Anchor a lie to a verifiable truth and a forged image/"cut content." |

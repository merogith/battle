# 05 — Technique Toolkit: The Reusable Craft Library

*Part of the story-research dossier. This file distills `01`–`04` into a system-agnostic toolkit of
narrative techniques the maintainer can apply per scene. Each technique states the move, why it works,
its best exemplar(s), and the in-game application surface. Cross-references: `[01]` canon, `[02]`
creepypasta, `[03]` fanfic/theories, `[04]` villain reception, `[06]` our narration map.*

> **The governing principle (from the user):** no tone ceiling — dark, adult, +18, gore, explicit
> sexual or violent content are all fair game *where the beat earns it* — but the rule above every
> technique is **preserve the narrative structure and flow that makes the source work, and polish
> what's there rather than bolt on shock that damages the narration.** Explicitness in service of the
> story, never at its expense. The recurring lesson of `[01]`/`[02]` is that **restraint and real
> consequence out-perform manufactured shock** (the *real* murdered Marowak beats "Lavender Town
> Syndrome"); when we do go explicit, it must be the *earned* version, not the loud one.

---

## A. Villain & antagonist craft (from `[01]`, `[04]`)

1. **The two-tier / dual villain — "the cause is the costume."**
   Run a *sincere figurehead* in front of a *cynical operator*; the front is so airtight the rank-and-file
   don't know the real plan. The reveal recontextualizes everything retroactively. *Exemplar:* N + Ghetsis
   `[01§1][04§4]`. *Surface:* villain arcs with a figurehead + hidden hand (plasma is the obvious fit;
   the technique can be imported into others).

2. **Let the villain be partly right; rebut by gameplay, not monologue.**
   Give the antagonist a real argument the text refuses to knock down cheaply — answer it through the
   player's own bond/verbs. *Exemplar:* N's "are we right to keep/battle Pokémon?" answered by play;
   Cyrus's self-refuting "I purged emotion" `[01§1,§3]`. *Surface:* boss pre-fight dialogue + the
   choice system reading back the player's earlier mercy/cruelty.

3. **The cold, sincere villain (anti-ham).** Calm, courteous, honest, never raises his voice — the
   flatness *is* the characterization; scarier than a screamer. *Exemplar:* Cyrus `[01§3][04§3]`.
   *Surface:* our galactic arc already does this; protect it from over-explaining.

4. **The tragic mirror.** Pair the cold operator with a tragic figure who carries the moral weight and
   whose mistake the villain is repeating, ignored. *Exemplar:* AZ ↔ Lysandre `[01§2]`. *Surface:*
   any villain arc can gain a "the one who already paid this price" NPC.

5. **Tone must match stakes; seed the real villain throughout; show the aftermath.** The cautionary
   trio: Flare (genocide + joke grunts + a pitying cast), Rose/Macro Cosmos (real villain crammed into
   the last 15 minutes), Lysandre (world forgets the near-apocalypse instantly). *Exemplars:* `[04§5,§7]
   [01§2]`. *Surface:* villain beat *pacing* across roads 2–7, and post-boss `ending` scenes that let
   consequences persist.

6. **Motive + accountability > new plot (the ORAS lesson).** The same villains went from forgettable
   to beloved purely by adding interiority and post-crisis accountability. *Exemplar:* Magma/Aqua →
   ORAS `[04§2]`. *Surface:* thin villain leaders gain the most from one interiority beat, not a bigger
   scheme.

7. **Sympathetic-from-the-start has a cost.** The post-Gen-7 "the team isn't really evil" trend
   (Skull, Star) raises resonance but lowers menace; fans split. A standout needs **both** a relatable
   human core **and** a credible source of dread. *Exemplar:* `[04§6,§8]`. *Surface:* our skull/star/
   yell arcs — keep the heart, add a real threat-vector.

## B. Horror craft (from `[02]`, `[01§5-6]`)

8. **Real-Pokédex-lore-turned-literal.** Don't invent dark lore — believe the canon entry and strip
   the cute buffer; the dissonance is the engine. *Exemplars:* Drifloon abducts children, Yamask's
   human face, Phantump, Banette, Parasect's puppeted corpse `[02 canon-dread]`. *Surface:* every
   extra arc is already built on this — push each to *fully* trust its entry.

9. **Familiar-made-wrong (twist one property).** Take a trusted asset/move/ritual and corrupt a single
   attribute (Flash → red glow). *Exemplar:* Lost Silver `[02]`. *Surface:* barks, status text, the
   battle log, the overworld of a raid.

10. **Diegetic-UI / save-file as narrative.** Tell the story through menus, party order, metadata; an
    almost-normal interface goes progressively wrong with no narrator. *Exemplars:* Lost Silver's
    acrostic party (LEAVE/DYING), the "DIED"-not-"fainted" vocabulary swap `[02]`. *Surface:* the
    Pokédex "handwriting you don't recognize," the rival journal, the save itself — strong fit for the
    loop arc.

11. **The death/faint vocabulary swap.** Strip the engine's protective euphemism for *one* high-impact
    reveal. *Exemplar:* Lost Silver `[02]`. *Surface:* a single bark or raid line; use once, never as
    wallpaper.

12. **The silent overworld.** Remove music/NPCs; stillness reads as wrongness. *Exemplar:* Lost Silver
    `[02]`. *Surface:* extra-arc raid framing.

13. **The second-person victim / relocated fourth wall.** The game/poem addresses *you* by name; or
    the player is placed inside the fiction's economy while something speaks to them from outside.
    *Exemplars:* Glitchy Red ("Nice try, [name]"), Hypno's Lullaby, DDLC, NieR-E `[02][03§6]`. *Surface:*
    the Mystery Figure / loop reveal is the natural home — it can address the *player's* persistence,
    not just the character's.

14. **Safe-container subversion.** Hide horror in the safest possible form/POV (lullaby, jingle,
    tutorial voice, a child's drawing). *Exemplar:* Hypno's Lullaby `[02]`. *Surface:* the phantump
    assembly song, the cubone letter, ambient/city-guide lines.

15. **The unwinnable encounter & crash-as-ending.** The only valid outcome is loss (mutual
    annihilation), or a "win" the system can't process. *Exemplars:* Buried Alive, Lost Silver's Perish
    Song + Destiny Bond `[02]`. *Surface:* a raid boss the design can frame as un-winnable in the
    fiction even when mechanically beaten.

16. **One forbidden verb / too-strong power with retroactive cost.** Give a power that's overtly OP and
    quietly atrocious; the cost lands *after* the player enjoyed it; the final target is the player's
    own avatar. *Exemplar:* Pokémon Black's CURSE/GHOST `[02]`. *Surface:* a villain/extra mechanic
    or a Master-Ball-style sink reframed morally.

17. **Perception-horror item & sanctioned uncanny space.** A ghost only visible with the right item
    (Silph Scope); a reverse-side dimension where physics is wrong (Distortion World) — both canon, no
    invention needed. *Exemplars:* `[01§5][02]`. *Surface:* extra-arc raids, the loop's "wrong" version
    of a familiar place.

18. **Credibility stack: official artifact + one true weird detail + (forged) proof.** Anchor dread to
    a verifiable truth, then add one anomaly. *Exemplar:* the construction of Lavender Town Syndrome
    `[02]`. *Surface:* loop breadcrumbs (a real sticker + one impossible detail).

## C. Mortality, loss & theme (from `[01]`)

19. **"Pokémon die" — puncture the loop's implicit immortality.** The most resonant canon repeatedly
    admits death (Lavender, the slain Marowak, Butterfree's farewell, the manga's on-page deaths and
    petrifications). Death gives the bond its weight. *Surface:* cubone/yamask/phantump arcs, victory/
    retreat lines, the loop's stakes.

20. **Love as relinquishment.** The genuinely moving beats are about *giving up* a Pokémon for its own
    good, inverting "catch 'em all." *Exemplars:* "Bye Bye Butterfree," "Pikachu's Goodbye," Floette
    leaving AZ `[01§2,§7]`. *Surface:* a release/letting-go beat; the plasma "uncage" choice.

21. **Emotion-deletion as the deepest fear.** The franchise's core horror is not death but the deletion
    of feeling — chosen (Cyrus) or inflicted (Shadow Pokémon). *Exemplars:* `[01§3,§6]`. *Surface:*
    galactic arc, and any "closed heart" raid framing.

22. **Adults failing children.** Cruelty flows downhill from adults onto the young or the made (N,
    Cubone, Mewtwo, Cyrus). *Surface:* nearly every arc already leans here — keep it the spine.

23. **Body-horror as the price of heroism.** Heroes frozen as statues while saving bystanders; sacrifice
    with a visible, lasting cost. *Exemplar:* the Adventures manga `[01§8]`. *Surface:* where we choose
    to spend explicit content, spend it on *cost*, not spectacle.

24. **Inversion of the core verb.** Reframe the central mechanic morally — "catch" → "rescue/heal"
    (Shadow Pokémon: snag + purify; PLA: soothe, don't slay). *Exemplars:* `[01§4,§6]`. *Surface:*
    raid/boss framing; the choice system.

## D. Meta & time-loop craft (from `[03]` — the main arc's home)

25. **The single load-bearing detail.** One concrete, slightly-wrong fact licenses the audience to build
    the whole structure — *plant it, don't explain it.* *Exemplar:* Lt. Surge's "the war" `[03§1]`.
    *Surface:* the "Welcome Back" sticker, the unfamiliar handwriting — we already have these; make one
    *undeniable*.

26. **Negative space as authorship.** What you *don't* show (absent adults, a town one notch too empty)
    lets players author the dread. *Exemplar:* `[03§5]`. *Surface:* city-guide ambient, road framing.

27. **The anomaly that demands explanation — then confirm it.** A persistent unexplained quirk breeds
    the "we've done this before" intuition; a loop game can do what the anime won't — *confirm it*,
    turning a fan-bug into canon. *Exemplar:* Ash's Coma `[03§4]`. *Surface:* the Mystery Figure reveal.

28. **Diegetic save/reset, morally costed.** Make remembering-across-resets a *character trait*, not a
    menu function; persistent state across loops gives the reveal teeth. *Exemplars:* Undertale,
    DDLC, NieR-E `[03§6]`. *Surface:* `main.loop.remember`, `sm.storyChoices`/`storyEventsFired`
    carried forward.

29. **The 80/20 loop anchor + one new piece per cycle.** Keep ~20% absolutely constant so repetition
    reads as intentional; reveal exactly one new fact each pass — a puzzle, not a GIF. *Exemplar:*
    `[03§6]`. *Surface:* the run-to-run breadcrumb cadence.

30. **The form is the theme.** Make the loop itself feel like a comfort/prison the protagonist built, so
    leaving it costs. *Exemplars:* OMORI, Stanley Parable `[03§6]`. *Surface:* the loop framing and the
    forget-vs-remember choice.

31. **Sacrifice forward to a stranger.** Players feel deeply about seeding a *future* run at their own
    cost — "breadcrumbs for the next loop" *is* this. *Exemplar:* NieR Ending E `[03§6]`. *Surface:*
    the ending choice; make the beneficiary (the next self / a stranger) and the cost real.

32. **Foreknowledge + selectively shared memory; consistency you can game; reward the return.** The
    loop rules should be consistent enough to reason about, the protagonist chooses whom to let in, and
    tiny consistent changes in familiar characters become the payoff across cycles. *Exemplars:* Ashes
    of the Past, Origin of Species, Traveler `[03§7]`. *Surface:* rival/Oak/Mystery Figure across loops.

33. **Anchor lasting reveals in what the game itself owns.** Cross-text inference is powerful but
    *deniable* (Fuji/Amber got retconned). *Exemplar:* `[03§3]`. *Surface:* keep the loop's load-bearing
    proof inside our own systems (the sticker, the journal), not borrowed lore.

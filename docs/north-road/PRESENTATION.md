## 1. Production sequence

1. Assemble one representative crop, prop pair, and touch layout for the flood lamp scene. Assemble one raid entry and one active warning for Marowak. Use the existing storytelling and battle presentation components.
2. Compare the scenes at 320×568, 568×260 with larger text, 820×1180 and 1440×900. Check that a newcomer can describe the scene and its actionable clue in all views. Record original art drafts outside the shipping asset tree.
3. Approve the pilot art kit, then author each city’s landmark and one actual payoff prop. Validate the stable geography, original art rights and no missing-asset path before preparing the finale.
4. Build the finale from four reusable visual states. Compose and mix the motif in the pilot, then lock the final audio stems against the actual transitions.
5. Run the installed package and reduced-motion versions. Repair broken composition, clipping, hidden focus, surprising volume and any missing clue in mute. Compare added transfer and installed bytes with Work 01.

### Framing and file acceptance

- Start wide illustrations at about 1280 × 720 and measure the actual encoded WebP. Current story art ranges roughly 60–205 kB per image. First pilot target is ≤150 KiB per wide still and ≤40 KiB per small prop where visual quality allows. A second portrait crop is justified by a genuine missing subject at the smallest target, and should use a smaller local export.
- Keep key action in a center-safe region and leave a calm text zone. On narrow portraits use art above or behind the title at stable low contrast. In short landscape let text and action take priority over art. Small devices retain the button, focus indicator, and route label.
- Lazy-load optional artwork at the current scene or when the relevant cached availability is confirmed. Include verified release files in the asset manifest. Show a readable plain environment if a fetch fails or the player only has the lightweight scene treatment.
- Art marks the story’s physical state. Use text, alt or a short visible caption for evidence essential to choice. Decorative sprites and overlays should not repeat an already spoken name to assistive technology.
- Compare actual foreground bytes, warm/cold caching, decode time, contrast and cropped composition in both orientations before passing. Apply earlier app startup budgets as targets to validate, with the pilot adding no eager media load. [Delivery plan](../ARENA_OVERHAUL_PLAN.md#7-offline-play-installs-and-updates)

## 4. Audio and motion specification

Compose an original short four-note shape that can plausibly live in the region. Start with a restrained plucked or bell sound and a low wind bed. Echo the same relative phrase through the road relay, take music down for one beat when meeting The First, and resolve it in the gallery. Choose the actual motif by reducing ambiguous overlap with UI cues during mix review. The player’s own partner still uses the established species cry flow.

| Cue | Trigger | Duration and mix starting point | Accessibility and interruption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Partner greeting | Committed starter or the quiet final image | Within three seconds, original cue heard over lower playback bed | Muted music suppresses the motif. The visible partner and frame information remain. No duplicate start after a restored reply. |
| Flood lamp / field relay | Front of a relevant scene and after a genuine relight | One dry mechanical relight and a separate quiet radio texture. Both under two seconds. | Optional caption such as “The lamp comes back on.” Signal visuals and labels give the same essential evidence. |
| Raid tell and expiry | Native phase announcement, activation or actual end | One short distinct onset. Keep expiry softer. Rate-limit repeated identical cues from animation or log redraw. | A named icon and text persist for the real duration. Use no screen shake under reduced motion. Never delay a native turn waiting for audio. |
| Rare sighting and reward | Genuine sighting or committed capture | Use separate setup and resolution. Keep cry clear of the musical attack. | One captioned text channel. Settings Stop/Mute clears the playing and queued cues. |
| Gallery transition | Arrival at the final story choice | Short musical statement, quiet at the first choice. Test phone speaker balance against battle cues. | Respect browser audio gesture and muted/paused tracks. Resume history can render with no sound and still be complete. |

Route new cues through the existing AudioSystem and StoryFx ownership. One background music owner plays at a time. Existing generated and file modes keep their working volume and mute. Effects honor their own switch. Keep sound separate from persistence, gameplay RNG and deadlines. Test changing preferences during a cue, audio disabled from first launch, a suspended phone resumed mid-scene, warping speed/skip through a scene, and device audio focus loss.

Use fades, small transforms and layered stills. Honor reduced motion and the game’s own animation setting together. Instant mode promptly displays clear final presentation. Skip waits for a committed result if a real decision is in flight, then reaches its lawful interaction or restores visible controls. Give focus to the newly opened dialog, return it when closed, and announce the result once instead of the last several animation frames. ## 5. Final asset and scene acceptance

- Four key cinematics and one optional evidence scene fit at minimum viewport and larger text. All visible essential controls accept touch and keyboard, Escape returns focus, and speaker labels stay understandable.
- Each new landmark, prop, portrait and cue has a source, compressed export, fallback and budget entry. Disconnected browser and missing-asset fixtures preserve the whole action and the meaning of the clue.
- Mystery victory and accepted loss differ at the correct branch. Both true finale choices show the saved partner or neutral fallback, produce exactly one result, and survive reload at each committed step.
- At least one listener on phone speakers and one on headphones can explain which sound was a cue and which supported atmosphere. They also complete the scene with sound disabled.
- The new asset paths appear in the verified package manifest. Package hash and bytes are captured after the final media exports. [Verification](RELEASE.md)

## 6. World presentation plan

Build North Road as an illustrated adventure with a small optional Journey Atlas. Give each place a landmark the player can recognize from its approach, town and local battle venue. Show the same place again when someone, something or its use changes. Let the chosen lead Pokémon share that space with the player. This direction fits the shipped paths, decisions, fixed-level progression, seeded encounters and competitive battle engine.

An atlas-first redesign would make travel options easy to compare, but would put a new navigation screen in front of the character scenes. A walkable tile map would give players more spatial agency, with a substantially larger art, input, collision and save-state workload. Prioritize illustrated places with direct actions and make the atlas useful for orientation, known habitats and existing return paths. The Pallet pilot is where the team decides how much spatial detail each road needs.

The camp already has partner selection, bond rewards and timing-free inputs. Its current result renderer largely returns text over a gradient. Carry the selected sprite into the result and make seeing the partner react part of the reward. ### Shared visual and interaction rules

1. **Compose the place first.** Each wide scene gets a recognizable silhouette, a low-detail area for controls, a foreground actor or object and one local action. Keep the existing illustrated world and crisp retro creature sprites. Look to Game Boy framing for clean silhouettes and Switch games for modern detail on demand. Keep early market windows, kitchens and Center lights genuinely inviting so the later silence and damaged places register.
2. **Use one scene layout with different art direction.** Use a shared place or activity header, a stage, a stable action region and a predictable Back or close. On a phone, use a shallow art crop and show the primary action in view. Use side-by-side art and controls where width permits. Reserve readable modern type for dialogue and controls and pixel type for brief labels where legible. A scene change can use an unobtrusive fade. Opening party details should retain stable navigation and scroll position.
3. **Use one prominent action.** Put the current required action first. Nearby people and optional leads follow, with already visited services organized by purpose. An arriving message, portrait or hint occupies reserved space. Read dialogue stays read on return. One consistent secondary treatment covers locks, costs, and finished states with text and icons as well as color.
4. **Give actions a sensory result.** Confirm, purchase, reject, find, learn and catch each get a recognizable visual and optional short sound. For an earned result, show what changed and a single relevant action. Use familiar controls for standard actions and recognizable objects to make the scene specific, such as a discharge chart at the Center or a target frame at the Dojo.
5. **Give the world a memory.** Attach a scene variation to an existing arrival, decision, chapter or encounter flag. Reuse one base with a changed prop, actor, light or one-line response. A revisited market can display returned tags next to the keeper. A lantern that was repaired stays lit while the same campaign state applies. Derive cosmetic motion separately from gameplay randomness, and keep camera, sound and art loading independent of saves and input.

## 9. Sound, music and background motion

- **One profile per place or venue.** Use the current place key to resolve art, foreground props, an ambient sound bed, a regional music section and the nearest fallback. Start with six sound families across coast, quarry/forest, wetlands, industry, highlands/chapel and League. Differentiate cities in a family with one short signature and a different arrangement or silence. Aim for reusable short seamless loops and short mono cues. Match the gain across families on a phone speaker and in headphones.
- **Let the music travel with the player.** Keep the region’s musical position when opening Bag, Dex or a connected utility. Use the right room tone for the local service. Switch to an entrance or battle track only for the actual encounter class. Restore the field position on return and respect user speed, mute and background settings. Ordinary fights resolve quickly. Rival, Gym and boss moments receive an intentional entry, a result, and a controlled pause where appropriate.
- **Compose around the current theme.** Reuse the four-note first-partner, field-relay and portrait arrangements. Bring it back through a real partner reaction, the weather relay and the final room. Give the League and important raids a stronger push into existing stings, with quieter stretches before major reveals. Keep creature cries, key hit or capture confirmation and clearly labeled optional story voice intelligible. The current synthetic terminal treatment fits recorded machinery and PA roles. Any new spoken line gets the same caption and proof of voice reuse permission.
- **Deduplicate and respect controls.** Start with the existing audio owner. Limit continuous playback to one score and one ambience bed, with bounded one-shots. Give dialogue and important confirmation priority and rate-limit repeat action noise. Deduplicate from actual scene or battle events. Suspend background activity appropriately, respect the initial browser gesture, and stop or release healthy audio resources on mute, disposal or route change. Required audio clues also have equivalent visual or textual information. [Audio system](../../battle.html#L15161) · [Existing story audio and voices](../../ATTRIBUTION.md#audio-assets)
- **Keep living scenes inexpensive.** Use sparse sprite poses, contained transforms and occasional environmental action. Freeze hidden or exited screens and use a strong authored still in reduced motion or when optional files are missing. Keep controls stable across image decoding, animation and dialogue arrival. Ordinary item, room and menu navigation gets immediate input feedback.

### Player, compatibility and performance verification

- Script five playstyles for the full matrix. A newcomer discovers where to go and trains a partner. An experienced builder uses filters and exact choices. An explorer comes back to a changed town and finds a secret from an in-world clue. A collector catches forms and traces a habitat through the Dex.

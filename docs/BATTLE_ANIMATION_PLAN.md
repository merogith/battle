# Battle presentation, cinematics, and pacing plan

## Goal

Make the whole encounter feel deliberate, from the first sign of danger through the return to exploration. Give routine fights clear, brisk starts and ends. Give Gym sequences real breathing room, and give major encounters a stronger sense of place, escalation, and consequence. Each playable move gets choreography that fits its role. The player should see who acted, where the move went, what happened, and when they can act again.

The battle engine remains the authority for results, requests, timers, and randomness. The work below changes how the game presents those results across Story, local battles, Competitive Arena, and online play.

The current implementation includes saved Gym intermissions, tiered encounter and result framing, the shared move presenter, and the optional first voice pack. The catalog includes 64 hand-authored move profiles, covering every move named in the raid scripts, and 890 profiles derived from move mechanics. Without counting color, the catalog has 511 distinct combinations of motion and timing. A browser check verifies that every move renders and releases its stage. Artistic review of individual effects and recorded character performances remain production work. The first voice pack has 13 original synthetic announcer, relay, and terminal lines. [Move audit](../scripts/audit-battle-move-profiles.mjs) · [Move gallery for local development](../scripts/debug/battle-motion-preview.html) · [Playtest protocol](PLAYER_TESTING_PROTOCOL.md)

[Battle renderer and settings](../battle.html)
- The native presenter writes log entries ahead of the effects and refreshes the HUD and switch-in sprites after a batch. One older damage path starts target effects before immunity checks. Standalone Competitive Arena redraws sprites and logs from the latest snapshot. Those paths need different adapters into the same presentation layer. [Main battle screen](../battle.html) · [Competitive Arena](../competitive/arena.js)
- Gyms 1–4 have one trainer before the leader. Gyms 5–8 have two. A trainer win already grants rewards, advances the story row, and opens a victory card with **Continue** and an **Up next** preview. **Continue** returns control to the story queue, which can immediately begin the next introduction. - The current game already has first-time Vibe Exchange, raid and rare-wild intros, boss phase banners, capture scenes, loss coaching, and milestone actions. In a local Chromium smoke test at a 390×844 viewport with motion enabled, the same hotseat Thunderbolt and Amnesia turn returned control in 583 ms in Normal, 264 ms in Fast, and 64 ms in Instant. The computed HP fill width transition was 800 ms in all three runs. This was one run per speed on desktop hardware. The device and move matrix in Phase 0 sets the baseline for release targets.

## The encounter from approach to return

| Beat | What the player sees | Implementation rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approach | Where they are, who's waiting, and one reason this fight matters. A track in the mud, a spectator leaving, or a scout's hint can do the work. | Use the active route, city, NPC, and saved flags. Show the actual event's title and public role. Fold a cold open into existing story, wild, or raid framing when they already share the same moment. |
| Entrance | The opposing source appears, the player's lead answers, and the battlefield comes into focus. Major first encounters get an authored portrait, sound cue, and one short line. | Complete the handoff into the first legal battle command. In normal offline launches, let incidental animation finish over usable controls. Keep existing engagement choices and any real pre-battle effects explicit. |
| Fight | Strong move silhouettes and timing, readable reactions, with one optional beat on a meaningful change. | Story phase effects and dialogue follow public engine events. An ace, form change, final slot, or threshold gets a beat only when it actually happens. Never require a turn-by-turn dialogue advance. |
| Resolution | Final confirmed action, a brief quiet beat where warranted, then the result, receipts, and one prominent next action. Defeat has its own restrained treatment. | Commit outcome before the presentation claims success. Wins, defeats, draws, retreat, forfeit, capture, escape, and disconnect have different labels. Only show earned rewards and verified observations. |
| Recovery | The player decides when to leave the result. A chain offers a quiet moment to prepare. A major story fight shows one visible consequence before the chapter moves on. | Use one player-paced surface for receipt, story bridge, and continuation. Optional deeper dialogue and journal entries stay off the critical path. The next offline fight never starts while the player is still on the result screen. |

Nintendo describes Gyms that express their leader's personality before the match and stadium entrances that make Gym Leaders feel like events. Valve's pacing talk describes using peaks and recovery periods to create contrast and distinguishes pacing from changing difficulty. Apply those principles to Battle's locations and existing fights. Fast uses shorter transitions and skips repeat decoration by default. Instant displays essential information without motion waits. Reduced motion applies to entrances, battle actions, results, and dialogue cards. Use composed stills rather than movement where framing alone conveys the beat. None of these modes bypasses an existing story choice, actual match decision, or the Gym player-paced break.

For a major win in Normal, let the last attack's impact finish, hold a short still as the opponent reacts, and carry the arena into the outcome card with a single sound cue. Introduce the badge or equivalent reward once the save confirms it. A loss has a quieter downbeat and recognizable exit back to Retry or town. Retain the actual final scene for draws and forfeits. A rare catch starts with the confirmed throw, resolves at the ball's stop, then opens the correct Party or PC receipt beside **View in Pokédex** and **Continue**. A breakout returns to the actions that actually remain available.

## Gym intermission and checkpoint

Implement this inside the existing result and town surfaces. Use one stable title, progress indicator, outcome line, and primary action. Keep optional actions secondary. The result screen becomes the breather after a trainer, avoiding a second mandatory results dialog.

1. **After the trainer falls**, hold the final frame briefly in Normal, then show **Trainer cleared · 1 of 2** on a two-fight Gym, or **Trainer cleared · 1 of 3** in a three-fight Gym. Show earned rewards and one original reaction that acknowledges the player's advance. Display **Team ready for the next match** when the existing recovery rule applies. Keep the HUD's actual battle-end HP distinct from the next match's readiness.
2. **If another trainer is due**, use **Next trainer**. A short line can distinguish what changes next, such as a different style of defense when supported by already disclosed or earned scouting information. Otherwise use a location or character line. **Review team** exposes the current lead and roster without opening the opponent's sealed build.
3. **Before the leader**, change the emphasis and primary action to **Face the leader**. Show a portrait or stage detail and one story link or known tactical clue. Start the existing major introduction and Vibe Exchange only where eligible. Use one deliberate confirmation for the fight, with a skip path to the same valid ready state. Preserve any mechanical banter choice and its existing effects.
4. **Let players take a longer break.** **Back to town** returns to the same Gym hub. **Resume Gym** shows the current round and reopens the checkpoint. Eligible town facilities, party restrictions, item rules, and bans follow the current run. A voluntary break between cleared fights keeps those wins and grants, and costs nothing. The separate live-battle retreat and defeat continue to use their existing rules and costs. Reopening the intermission keeps any existing locked matchup and the ordinary seeded upcoming matchup source.
5. **After the leader**, show the committed badge, one usable reward or unlock, and the chapter's next action. For an earned party slot, offer the existing **Add from PC** when a reserve is available. Route the existing City 5 move-power milestone to one legal Tutor preview when it actually unlocks. Route players to the next town or road according to the committed row. A loss offers the current retry and retreat choices, with one helpful observed detail when available.

### Save and recovery contract

- Add a versioned pending presentation receipt in the story save. Use a stable attempt identity plus completed and next event row IDs, not array positions. Store outcome, actual reward display, safe next actions, and aftermath identity as values. Resolve names and text through current data. Keep narration and reward generation independent, and use the existing validated encounter and reward records.
- Record the completed row, receipts, and progress in a single authoritative story save before showing the result as saved. Existing capture receipts provide a model for commit and rollback. Keep any separately persisted achievement writes idempotent with the same receipt or reconcile them after the story commit. If storage fails, stay on a recoverable result with **Retry save**. Retrying the write uses the same staged outcome with no re-roll, reward grant, or fight launch.
- On resume, resolve unacknowledged result or intermission before the normal event router. Acknowledgement or leaving for town is idempotent. Resume from town preserves the same next fight. Reload at any point before or after next-fight launch yields either the committed intermission or the existing in-battle checkpoint path. Validate save migration and corrupt or stale receipts against authoritative progress. Guard double taps, queued clicks, and cross-tab ownership so an earlier screen cannot reopen, start, or pay for a resolved round again.
- Recognize a committed result in close autosave before the active-fight retreat check. In the previous flow, a win could leave the current row pointing at another battle after the active checkpoint was cleared. Closing from that result could follow the Hard or Very Hard retreat path. Test that closing or leaving a won intermission preserves the receipt and costs nothing. [Current close autosave logic](../battle.html#L65849)
- Reuse each mode's own persistence. Safari, Fight Club, Frontier, Crucible, Gauntlet, and online keep their existing checkpoints, stakes, and row ownership. Do not create Story progress in off-timeline modes. Apply the same presentation contract around their outcome and resume paths. ## Design for the battle screen

1. **Show the cause.** Give the acting Pokémon a short, appropriate pose or motion. A close strike moves toward the target. A beam starts at the source. A heal lands on the Pokémon that received it. A hazard settles where it remains active. Give self, side, field, and target effects distinct footprints.
2. **Show the result at contact.** Tie the reaction, initial HP change, important battle-log line, and sound accent to a shared event. A miss passes the target. Protect catches the attack at the shield. Immunity, absorption, and a hit to Substitute have their own responses. Use text and symbols alongside color and sound.
3. **Keep inputs and information steady.** Add an effects layer inside the arena with source, target, feet, side, and field anchors. Keep commands, HP and status labels, and the log in fixed positions. The layer takes no pointer input. On compact screens, shorten travel paths and reduce particle size and motion while retaining the recognizable shape and rhythm. Mirror anchors for the opposite player perspective.
4. **Make the arena respond sparingly.** Add a ground shadow and small pose changes for weight. Use restrained weather or terrain detail behind the action. Reserve framing changes, shadow shifts, and changes in music for introductions, phase changes, or other moments where the player has something new to notice. Major defeats, switch-ins, and pursuit or capture beats should have different endings.

For visual research, use Pokémon Champions as a cross-device framing reference and Riot's effects guidance for keeping the most important action legible. Use original effects and choreography that fit Battle’s atmosphere. [Pokémon Champions gameplay](https://champions.pokemon.com/en-gb/gameplay/) · [Riot Games visual effects](https://www.riotgames.com/en/artedu/visual-effects)

## Playback and speed

Use the saved **Normal**, **Fast**, and **Instant** choices. Keep form timing and sound preferences working, and apply the effective battle speed consistently to every battle cue. Pilot timings are measured from the first visible cue to the end of the move's blocking presentation. Validate them against real turns on phones before freezing them.

| Setting or action | Pilot behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Normal, light action | 250–350 ms for a quick strike or small status cue. |
| Normal, standard action | 400–650 ms for a typical attack, heal, or field action. |
| Normal, signature action | 750–1,100 ms for selected moves with an authored buildup and payoff. |
| First-time story phase reveal | 1.2–1.8 seconds in Normal, immediately skippable. Repeat visits use a short beat. |
| Fast | About half of Normal. Compress wind-up and recovery first, leaving a readable contact or block. Group long sequences and bypass extended story reveals. |
| Instant | Apply authoritative visible changes and log in the next available render once results are ready. Remove animation waits and use only short permitted feedback. |
| Reduced motion | Honor the operating system and game settings at every speed. Use static silhouettes or gentle fades, a readable result marker, and optional sound in place of camera moves, shake, and large motion. |

Start the action announcement with its opening cue. Overlap HP easing with recovery. Allow cosmetic tails to finish in the background when input is ready. A real replacement prompt, additional target choice, or unsettled game state is a reason to wait. Give players a clear skip control during long sequences, keep keyboard focus stable, and make switching to Instant finish the current visual queue cleanly. Online timers and available choices follow their authoritative request even while an effect is playing. Keep full-screen strobes out of the design and check rapid chained effects for flashing. [W3C flashing guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)

For a 450 ms projectile in Normal, a first pass could spend 90 ms on the pose and announcement, 130 ms on travel, and then start the reaction, audio, and HP change together at 220 ms. Recovery runs through 450 ms, with any lingering fragments clearing independently. Check the same shot after an immunity, on a narrow screen, and at Fast before using it as a template.

## Give each move its own identity

Build reusable effect families for contact, projectiles, waves, beams, terrain, shields, healing, traps, and other common behaviors. Give every enabled canonical move ID an authored profile using those pieces. The profile describes the pose, direction or footprint, silhouette or material, rhythm, impact, sound accent, and aftermath. Each move needs a meaningful visual distinction from its closest neighbors. Recoloring alone is insufficient. Use actual move targeting and resolution rules, including physical moves that make no contact and special moves that do.

| Move | Proposed direction |
| --- | --- |
| Tackle | Close the gap with a short body check, a blunt impact, and a footfall on recovery. |

| Fake Out | A sharp feint and abrupt pop. Show a flinch response only when the resolved event confirms it. |

## Implementation and library choice

Use a cancelable player that consumes resolved battle events from each surface. Give it an ordered presentation cursor and snapshots of visible HP, statuses, identities, and placements at each event. Cue the visible state at contact while the simulator holds the authoritative latest state. Online and spectator adapters consume viewer-safe events and never derive appearance, ability, item, alternate form, or exact HP from hidden information. Replace repeat or stale event IDs safely after resync. A replay or retry clears motion and sound from the previous battle. ## Release checks

- **Simulation and ordering.** Fixed seed plus fixed choices yields the same outcome, RNG progression, public log, HP, status, PP, and legal requests across speeds, reduced motion, skip, and renderer fallback. Validate switch-in KOs, U-turn replacement before a queued attack, double faint, Illusion, spread targeting where supported, and reconnect or resync. Check retry, replay, quit, and background and resume for stale effects and duplicate log or audio cues.
- **Responsiveness.** Measure median and p95 time from the final submitted choice to the next valid input, plus impact timing and frame times. Pilot goals are roughly half of Normal turn presentation time in Fast and no scripted delay in Instant. For locally resolved Instant turns, target p95 of 100 ms from resolved request to usable controls on the reference phone. Network time is measured separately. Aim for smooth 60 Hz on the reference device and stable 30 Hz in low detail. Drop cosmetic density before adding delays.
- **Data and lifecycle.** Start with budgets of 60 KiB compressed additional critical code and 250 KiB compressed new assets during a typical early battle, both relative to the current build. Review after the baseline. Measure warm repeat, fresh install, save and retry, version upgrade, interrupted download, offline training, and a rare move when optional art is absent. Profile a long battle for DOM, memory, and audio cleanup.
- **Gym and story.** Test all twelve Gym trainer result transitions, eight leader victories, and representative losses and retries in both Gym formats. Verify both Glasswater intermissions, deliberate Continue, team review, free voluntary town exit, Hard close/reopen, failed saves, stale receipt recovery, receipt acknowledgement, and the same locked encounter on resume. Review at least one full seed per variable track and automated coverage for every chapter and track outcome. Check final-climax identity and private evidence through replays, archive, and offline fallbacks. [Current story hierarchy fixture](../tests/visual/story-cinematic-hierarchy.mjs) · [Story milestone actions](../tests/visual/story-milestone-actions.mjs)
- **Screens and accessibility.** Extend existing 11-viewport tests with full-motion fixtures and capture wind-up, impact, and aftermath. Include 320×568 portrait, 568×260 landscape, tablet, desktop, safe areas, rotation mid-action, touch, keyboard, and reduced motion. Review Chrome, Firefox, Safari, real iOS, and Android. Verify that controls and essential outcome information stay readable and available over the busiest effects. [Existing battle layout coverage](../tests/visual/battle-responsive.mjs) · [Native pivot coverage](../tests/visual/classic-native-pivot.mjs) · [Online room coverage](../tests/visual/online-native-room.mjs)
- **Audio and story voice.** Verify zero unused voice transfers, mute and settings persistence, blocked autoplay, audio interruptions, missing assets, offline install, long or unexpectedly quick dialogue advances, and no stale speech after resuming or changing rooms. Listen on phone speakers and headphones in both existing music modes, and confirm muted captions convey the encounter. [Existing story audio fixture](../tests/visual/story-audio.mjs)
- **Player review.** Give new and experienced players an early Gym, a later three-fight Gym, and a story boss. Observe whether they choose when to start, understand what changed, notice the relevant story beat, and find the correct next action. Ask what happened after a miss, shield, immunity, swap, and multi-hit. Log which moments the player skips or waits through, including repeated rematches. Use the same turns at Normal and Fast, plus a muted pass, and revise before authoring the rest of the catalog.
